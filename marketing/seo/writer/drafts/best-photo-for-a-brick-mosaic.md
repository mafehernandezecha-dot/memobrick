# Best Photo for a Brick Mosaic: 7 Simple Tips

The best photo for a brick mosaic is clear and well lit, with the subject large in the frame and a simple background behind it. You do not have to guess, because the website shows your photo as a mosaic before you order.

Below are seven simple tips for choosing a photo, advice for group, couple and pet photos, and the photos to avoid or fix first.

## How a photo becomes a brick mosaic

A custom brick mosaic turns your photo into a grid of small squares. Each square becomes one 1×1 brick in one of 40+ colors. Up close you see the bricks. From a few steps back, you see the photo.

That is why some photos work better than others. Fine detail, like a tiny face in a wide scene, gets fewer bricks. Big, clear shapes and good contrast come through well.

## What makes a photo work: 7 tips

Use this checklist when you scroll through your phone.

1. Clear faces and good light.
2. The subject fills the frame.
3. A simple, uncluttered background.
4. Strong contrast between the subject and the background.
5. A sharp photo, not a blurry one.
6. The original photo, not a screenshot.
7. A genuine expression over a perfect pose.

### 1. Clear faces and good light

Soft, natural light is your friend. A photo taken near a window or outdoors on a bright day usually works well. Avoid heavy shadows across faces, like a hat brim or harsh midday sun, because shadows can hide eyes and smiles.

### 2. Let the subject fill the frame

Crop in close. The bigger the faces are in the photo, the more bricks each face gets, and the more it looks like the person you love. A wide shot of a beach with two tiny people in the middle will mostly show sand and sky.

### 3. Pick a simple background

A plain wall, a clear sky or a soft, blurred background keeps the focus on the people or pet. Busy backgrounds, like a crowded room or a patterned sofa, compete with the faces.

### 4. Look for strong contrast

The subject should stand out from what is behind it. Dark hair against a light wall, or a light-colored dog on a dark rug, reads clearly. When the subject and background are close in color, they can blend together.

### 5. Choose a sharp photo

Pick the sharpest version you have. If you took a burst of photos, choose the one where the eyes are in focus. Motion blur and out-of-focus faces get softer, not clearer, once they become bricks.

### 6. Use the original, not a screenshot

Upload the original photo from your phone or camera when you can. A screenshot, a photo sent through a messaging app, or a photo of a printed picture or screen often loses quality along the way.

### 7. A real smile beats a perfect pose

The photo that matters most is often the one with a real laugh or a candid moment. A gift made from that moment means more than a stiff pose, as long as the faces are clear.

## Group photos, couples and pets

### Group photos

Fewer people usually read better, because each face gets more bricks. Larger sizes fit more people. For the smaller sizes, upload your photo and check the preview. To compare every size, see our [brick mosaic size guide](https://mymemobrick.com/blogs/news/brick-mosaic-sizes).

### Pets

Get down to their eye level and fill the frame with their face. Make sure the eyes are visible, since they bring the mosaic to life. A light-furred pet looks great against a darker background, and a dark-furred pet against a lighter one.

### Couples and anniversaries

A close photo of two faces usually beats a wide scene. If your favorite photo is a big landscape from a trip, try cropping in on the two of you and compare the previews. ## Photos to avoid or fix first

- **Very dark photos.** Brighten the photo a little before you upload it, or choose one taken in better light.
- **Heavy filters.** Strong color filters change skin tones and colors. The original usually looks more natural.
- **Tiny faces in a big scene.** Crop in on the people first.
- **Busy patterns.** Stripes, crowded backgrounds and lots of small objects can distract from the faces.

## FAQ

### What is the best photo for a brick mosaic?

A clear, well-lit photo with the subject large in the frame and a simple background. Sharp faces and good contrast between the subject and the background help most.

### Can I use a group photo?

Yes. Fewer people read better, and larger sizes fit more. The product pages list 1 to 3 people for 40×20, up to 1 to 6 people for 40×50. Use the preview to check your photo.

### Can I use a photo of my dog or cat?

Yes. Choose a photo where the face fills the frame and the eyes are visible, with a background that contrasts with their fur.

Yes. Upload the original photo from your phone rather than a screenshot or a photo sent through a messaging app.
