# Brick Mosaics for Occupational Therapists and Special-Education Teachers

A Memobrick brick mosaic is a kit of 1×1 bricks, white baseplates and a printed color map that builds a photo into a picture, row by row. Some occupational therapists and special-education teachers may find it a useful, flexible material for their own goals, such as fine motor practice, following a visual sequence, or turn-taking. This page describes what the kit involves so you can judge whether it fits your work.

We are a gift company, not clinicians. We make no claims that a mosaic produces any therapeutic or educational outcome. You know your students and clients, and how to set and measure goals. For a plain-language summary of the research on brick building with autistic children, see our main guide to [brick mosaics as a calm, shared activity](https://mymemobrick.com/blogs/news/brick-mosaic-calm-activity-autistic-kids-adults).

## Goals you might choose to use it for

The examples below are written as possibilities, in the kind of language practitioners use. They are not recommendations or outcome claims. Whether a mosaic suits any goal is your professional judgment.

### Fine motor practice

The core action is picking up a small 1×1 brick and pressing it onto a stud. Depending on the person, you might use it to give practice with:

- Pincer grasp and picking up small objects.
- Controlled pressing and placement.
- Using the included tool to remove a brick.
- Working within a defined grid space.

### Following a visual sequence

The printed color map is a visual plan with one correct color for each spot. You might use it for:

- Matching a color on the map to a labeled bag.
- Scanning a row from left to right.
- Keeping one's place, with or without a ruler or strip under the row.
- Working through a long task in small, defined steps (one row, five rows, one baseplate).

### Turn-taking and cooperative work

The three roles from LEGO-based therapy (engineer, supplier, builder) adapt well to a mosaic: one person reads the map, one sorts colors, and one presses bricks. Our family guide to [building together as a reader, sorter and builder](https://mymemobrick.com/blogs/news/build-together-reader-sorter-builder) covers role cards, switching signals and turn markers.

### Task persistence and finishing

Progress is visible after every row, and there is a clear finish. Some practitioners may use row counts or baseplates as concrete, countable session targets.

## What the research says

No study has tested brick mosaics. The best-studied related approach is LEGO-based therapy, in groups of three with set roles. The largest trial, I-SOCIALISE (250 children aged 7 to 15, 98 UK schools, 12 weekly sessions), found a small improvement in teacher-rated social skills at 20 weeks that was not statistically significant in the main analysis (effect size 0.18, p = 0.06; 0.21, p = 0.036 in the per-protocol analysis). The difference was not seen at 52 weeks. The approach was found safe and cost-effective.

Sources: [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10576908), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36991578/), [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/e78f7727f8d74c3f8724c56c8cc066de).

## Practical planning notes

### Sensory set-up

Short sessions, one row at a time, colors sorted into trays, good even lighting and a soft mat under the baseplate are simple adjustments. Our [sensory-friendly building tips](https://mymemobrick.com/blogs/news/sensory-friendly-building-tips) list more.

### Choosing the picture

A subject connected to the group or to a student's special interest can make the build more engaging. Use only photos you have permission to use, and follow your school's or practice's rules on photos of students. Our guide to [turning a special interest into a brick mosaic](https://mymemobrick.com/blogs/news/special-interest-brick-mosaic) has photo tips.

### Making the design

You can make the design yourself in the [Memobrick editor](https://mymemobrick.com/pages/editor), see it as a mosaic, and compare sizes before ordering. If you prefer, Memobrick's designers can create it and send a [free proof](https://mymemobrick.com/pages/free-preview) before production. The proof is optional.

### Small parts

The kit contains many small bricks. It is not suitable for young children or for anyone who puts things in their mouth. Plan supervision and clean-up for your setting.

## FAQ

### Is a brick mosaic a therapy tool?

It is a creative kit, not a therapy product. Some practitioners may choose to use it as a material within their own sessions and goals. Memobrick makes no claims about therapeutic or educational outcomes.

### What fine motor actions does building involve?

Picking up small 1×1 bricks, pressing them onto studs, and using the included tool to remove bricks. Whether that fits a given goal is your professional judgment.

### Can several students work on one mosaic?

Yes. Sizes from 20×20 up are made of several baseplates, so students can work on different sections at the same time. A 30×30 has nine baseplates.

### Do you offer classroom or bulk pricing?

There is no set classroom price. Larger or multi-kit projects are quoted individually. Please contact us with what you need.

### Is the kit suitable for young children?

No. It contains many small bricks and is not suitable for young children or anyone who puts things in their mouth.

### Is there research on brick mosaics?

Not on mosaics. The closest research is on LEGO-based therapy, where the largest trial found a small improvement in teacher-rated social skills that was not statistically significant in the main analysis. See the research section above for details and sources.
