# Research brief: brick mosaics as a calm, shared activity for autistic kids and adults (2026-10-28)

Asked by Eden: "do a research how memobrick can help people maybe people with autism" and "write plenty ... maybe will bring new audiences".

## What the research says (cite these, word claims exactly this carefully)

- LEGO-based therapy (groups of 3: engineer reads instructions, supplier finds bricks, builder builds) is the best-studied brick activity for autistic children. Largest trial, I-SOCIALISE (250 children aged 7 to 15, 98 UK schools, 12 weekly sessions): small improvement in teacher-rated social skills at 20 weeks that was not statistically significant (effect size 0.18, p = 0.06; per-protocol 0.21, p = 0.036), not seen at 52 weeks; safe and cost-effective.
  Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10576908 , https://doaj.org/article/e78f7727f8d74c3f8724c56c8cc066de , https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36991578/
- Calm, focused daily practice (mindfulness, 10 to 15 minutes a day) reduced stress and anxiety for autistic adults in early studies (MIT-led study, 89 adults, 2025; 2021 review). No study tests brick mosaics themselves.
  Sources: https://mcgovern.mit.edu/brain-disorder/autism-spectrum-disorder , https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8362926/
- Visual, step-by-step supports and predictability are widely used autism supports (visual supports are on the National Autism Center's evidence-based list per secondary sources; not verified against the primary report).
  Source: https://www.autismparentingmagazine.com/creating-visual-schedules/
- Calming spaces: autistic adults want individualized choices, not one-size sensory toys (Autism in Adulthood survey, 2025).

## Why Memobrick fits (product facts, not health claims)

One brick size (1×1), a color map, the same motion repeated; the picture appears row by row with a clear finish; the subject is personal (pet, family, a favorite character: special interests motivate); can be built together (the three LEGO-therapy roles map onto reader, color sorter, builder) or alone; pressing small bricks.

## Hard rules

- Never claim Memobrick treats, improves, helps or is therapy for autism, anxiety, ADHD or anything medical. Allowed: calm, focused, predictable, something to build together, many families tell us (only with a real, permitted quote).
- Identity-first and person-first language both appear; follow autistic self-advocates' common preference ("autistic people") and say so once; never "suffers from", never pity, never "cure".
- Autistic people speak for themselves: no invented testimonials, no fake quotes, no AI people presented as autistic customers.
- Small parts: say the kit has small bricks and is not for young children who put things in their mouths; check `product-facts.md` for the age warning before stating an age.
- Not a sales hook on its own: lead with useful tips, the product second.
