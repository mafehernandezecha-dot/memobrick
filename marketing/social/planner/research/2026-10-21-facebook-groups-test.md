# Proposal: 2-week Facebook groups test (5 groups)

Agent: social-planner. Research: `marketing/social/planner/research/2026-10-21-facebook-groups.md`.

Approving this item starts nothing automatic. It lets Eden take part in 5 Facebook groups by hand, from her own profile (or the Memobrick Page where a group allows Pages), following the rules below, and lets the planner prepare one post kit per group. The Mac does nothing in groups unless a later, separate item approves it.

## Dates and steps

- **Step 0, read-only (Oct 23 to Oct 27):** Eden opens each group (or the Mac's Claude in Chrome does it read-only, logged in as Eden: no joining, no clicks on Join, no posts) and copies into `marketing/social/planner/research/facebook-groups-rules.md`: public or private, member count, posts per day (count yesterday's posts), the full rules, promo days, whether Pages may join, and whether links are allowed in posts or comments.
- **Week 1 (Oct 28 to Nov 3): join and listen.** Eden asks to join the 5 groups, answers the joining questions truthfully, and only reads and comments helpfully (about 3 real comments per group in the week). No Memobrick post.
- **Week 2 (Nov 4 to Nov 10): one post per group.** Wed Nov 4 Dog Mom Club, Thu Nov 5 Craft & Creative Ideas DIY, Fri Nov 6 Golden Doodle Owners, Sat Nov 7 Scrapbooking, Cards, & Crafts, Sun Nov 8 Cat Lovers Group Official (or the group's own promo day if it has one). Keep commenting helpfully, about 4 helpful comments for every Memobrick post. This week ends as demand doubles (week of Nov 10 last year).
- **Nov 11:** the planner reads Shopify and Eden's log and writes the result: which groups to keep, which to drop.

## Rules for every post

- Say who you are: "I run Memobrick, a small business that makes these kits." Never pose as a customer.
- Only real photos or videos: a mosaic Eden built, or clips marked `ok` in `marketing/social/footage-rights.md` (credit the creator if it is their pet or family). Never AI images in groups.
- No price, code or link in the post unless the group's rules allow it. Links only when someone asks, in the way the group allows, always tracked: `https://mymemobrick.com/pages/editor?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=group&utm_campaign=<slug>`.
- Never the same text or photo in two groups; at most one group post a day; never DM anyone who did not ask.
- Never use the word LEGO.
- Eden logs each day in `marketing/social/planner/research/facebook-groups-log.md` (posts, comments, questions, DMs, removals or warnings).

## Success

Keep a group if, by Nov 10, it brought at least 1 tracked order, or at least 30 tracked visits and 5 real questions, with no post removed and no warning. Stop the whole test early if any post is removed or Facebook shows any warning.

## Example posts

Each is written for the strictest likely rules (no links in the post). Eden adjusts it once that group's real rules are recorded in step 0.

### 1. The Dog Mom Club (national)

Fits a dog-owner group that usually allows dog photos and stories but not ads: it is a dog story first, says who Eden is, and has no link or price.

Photo: a real dog mosaic next to the photo it came from (for example the pink dog mosaic from the cleared Pink Butters folder, with the creator's OK to name her, or a dog mosaic Eden built).

> This is what happens when a dog photo becomes thousands of tiny bricks.
>
> Full disclosure: I run Memobrick, a small business that turns photos into brick mosaic kits you build at home. This one started as a phone photo, and every stud on it is a 1×1 brick placed by hand.
>
> My favorite part is the moment the face shows up halfway through the build. Dog moms, which photo of your pup would you turn into bricks?

### 2. Craft & Creative Ideas DIY

Fits a public craft group where finished projects and how-it-works posts are the normal content: it teaches the process and asks a craft question, no link.

Photos (4): the original photo, the printed color map, a board half built with hands placing bricks, the finished piece.

> Craft night project: turning a family photo into a brick mosaic, one 1×1 brick at a time.
>
> How it works: you start from a printed color map, sort the small bags of bricks by color, and press them onto the baseplate row by row. A 16×16 has 2,500 pieces and takes about 2.5 hours. It looks like random colors up close, and then you step back and there's the photo.
>
> I should say I run Memobrick, the small business that makes these kits, so I'm biased! Happy to answer any questions about the process. Has anyone else tried pixel-style crafts?

### 3. Scrapbooking, Cards, & Crafts

Fits a scrapbooking group where people share ways to keep and display photos: it is about the photo and the memory, not the product, and invites people to share their own.

Photo: a family photo next to its finished brick mosaic (from the cleared "Memobrick videos (couples, family, pet videos)" folder).

> A different way to keep a favorite photo.
>
> We scrapbook to keep the moments that matter, and this is another way to do it: a family photo turned into a mosaic of small 1×1 bricks that you build yourself and hang on the wall.
>
> Full disclosure, I run Memobrick, the small business behind these kits. I love seeing which photos people choose. If you could turn one photo from your albums into something you build by hand, which would it be?

Product details above match `marketing/shared/product-facts.md` (16×16: 2,500 pieces, 2.5 h building time per Shopify, 1×1 bricks, printed color map, color-sorted bags). Re-check in Shopify on the day of posting.
