## Who we are talking to

The real buyer (from `buyer-profile.md`, built from Shopify and Meta data on 2026-10-14): a US woman aged 35 to 64 buying a $70 to $150 personalized photo gift for her family, partner or pet, mostly for Christmas, then Valentine's Day and Mother's Day. Pick the angle for that reader, and pitch only outlets that pass the audience-fit check.

## The story in one breath

Memobrick turns a favorite photo into a brick portrait that the family builds together. The kit arrives, everyone gathers around the table, the bags of bricks open, and someone starts the first row. For a while it is just colors. Then, brick by brick, a face appears: a grin, a pair of eyes, the dog's ears. When the last brick goes in, the picture goes on the wall, and every time someone walks past it they remember the evening they built it.

Lead with that moment. The product details come second, as proof.

## The experience, beat by beat

1. **Choosing the photo.** The best part starts before the box arrives: the family picks the one photo that matters (the wedding kiss, grandpa with the grandkids, the dog as a puppy).
2. **The evening around the table.** Phones down, bricks sorted by color in small labeled bags, a printed color map, everyone with a job. Kids find colors, grown-ups place rows.
3. **The moment the face appears.** Up close it is just 1×1 bricks. Step back and the photo is there. That reveal, partway through the build, is the moment people remember.
4. **A keepsake that stays.** It is not a gift that gets used up. It hangs on the wall as a piece of art made from their own memory, built with their own hands.

## Angles by guide type

### Family and kids guides
- Hook: a family night that ends with a portrait on the wall.
- Points: kids sort colors and find their own face appearing; grown-ups place rows; the 20×20 (4,096 pieces, about 4 hours) is one evening or a weekend of short sessions; larger sizes split across baseplates so several people build at once.
- Gift ideas: grandparents with the grandkids' photo, a family vacation photo, the family dog.
- Photos: family on the wall (60x60 product image), child holding a portrait (20x20), baby portrait with size marks (20x20).

### Couples and wedding guides
- Hook: their favorite photo of each other, built together on a slow evening in the new home.
- Points: a creative date night rather than one more object; the engagement or wedding photo becomes art for the first home; the free design proof takes the guesswork out for a gift-giver who does not know the couple's preferences.
- Gift ideas: wedding gift, anniversary, first Christmas married.
- Photos: couple portrait on the wall (40x30), close-up of the brick texture (10x10), couple's pet (16x16).

### Mom, lifestyle, and general gift bloggers
- Hook: the gift for someone who has everything is a memory they get to rebuild.
- Points: covers every category on their list (women, men, pets, grandparents) because the photo can be anyone; a hands-on, screen-free activity; a keepsake rather than clutter; made and shipped from Miami; order by Dec 15 for Christmas.
- For review-based guides: offer a kit made from a photo of the reviewer's own family, so the review shows the real build moment.
- Photos: mom and daughter portrait (30x20), sisters with their dog (30x50), baby portrait (20x30).

## Pitch format

- Subject: warm and specific to the outlet, names the moment (for example "the night a family photo becomes a brick portrait").
- Opening (2 to 3 sentences): paint the building moment, then connect it to their guide by name.
- 2 to 3 talking points from the tables above, picked for that guide type.
- One ask: include Memobrick in the guide, with photos offered. Default: invite them to the creator program on Shopify Collabs (10% commission, 10% off code for their audience, apply at https://mymemobrick.com/pages/collabs). Offer a sample kit only for a KIT target (see kit-vs-commission-2026-10-14.md) and only with its own approved offer item.
- Sign-off may carry "As seen on FOX". Never "Fox News".
- Body under about 170 words. Sign "Eden Biri, Memobrick" with mymemobrick.com.
- Two versions: `pitches/<slug>.md` (plain text plus photo links) and `pitches/<slug>.html` (navy #1B264F, pink #D66A7E, white, hero photo, 2 small photos, signature). The HTML goes in the Gmail draft as `htmlBody` with the plain text as `body`; contact forms get the plain text with photo links.

## Autistic builders, their families, OTs and special-education teachers (added 2026-10-28)

Source and hard rules: `marketing/seo/briefs/2026-10-28-autism-calm-building-research.md`. Targets: `targets-2026-10-28.md`.

- **Never** say Memobrick treats, improves, helps or is therapy for autism, anxiety, ADHD or anything medical. No "fine motor practice", "regulation" or "reduces stress" claims either. Allowed words: calm, focused, predictable, something to build together or alone.
- Use identity-first language ("autistic people", "autistic builders"), the common preference of autistic self-advocates. Never "suffers from", never pity, never "cure".
- Autistic people speak for themselves: no invented quotes or reactions, no AI people presented as autistic customers. Ask autistic people for their view; do not speak for them.
- Product facts that fit (facts, not claims): one brick size (1×1); bricks in small labeled color bags; a printed color map; the same step repeated, row by row, with a clear finish; the subject is personal (a pet, a person, a favorite place); can be built alone or together (one reads the map, one sorts colors, one builds); the mosaic preview in the web editor is free with any photo.
- Always say: the bricks are small, so the kit is not for young children who still put things in their mouths. Do not state an age (no age warning in product-facts.md yet).
- Do not name characters or celebrities as subjects (IP risk). Say "a favorite place", "a pet", "a person".
- Asks by target type: creators and families get an honest-feedback ask with no obligation to post ("this isn't for me" is a useful answer); OTs and teachers get a request for their professional view of the format; gift guides get the holiday angle. Default is commission only (Collabs 10% and 10%); the no-kit ask is a free look at the editor preview. Kits only with evidence and their own offer item (see `kit-offers/README.md`).
- Do not pitch organizations that do not do product promotions (for example ASAN, AWN, NEXT for AUTISM's partner-only guide, Neurodivergent Insights' current partnership policy). Avoid tie-ins with ABA providers: many autistic self-advocates object to ABA.

## Lines to avoid

- Any press claim other than "As seen on FOX" or "Featured in FOX 4's holiday gift guide". Never "Fox News", never imply the coverage is recent, and never NBC, Yahoo, or Disrupt (not confirmed).
- A free kit for a commission-only target, or any commission or discount above 10%/10%.
- Customer counts, ratings, reviews, testimonials, or "loved by thousands".
- "Assembled", "we build it for you", or the pricier "Yes" option: only the build-at-home kit is offered.
- Faces per size for the 16×16 to 30×20 sizes (unknown).
- Delivery promises beyond "order by December 15 to receive before Christmas".
- Discount codes: none is active for press; the holiday 20% offer has no dates or code yet.
- Another brand's name for bricks or sets. Say "1×1 bricks" or "brick mosaic".
- "Designer-made" for a design the customer made in the editor. The free proof is optional.
- Offering payment, or anything that reads as a paid placement.
- Em dashes.
